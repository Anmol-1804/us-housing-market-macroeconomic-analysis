# Beyond the 2008 Crisis: How Did Macroeconomic Forces Reshape the US Housing Market?

## Project Overview
This project examines how the relationship between US housing prices and macroeconomic fundamentals changed following the 2008 Global Financial Crisis (GFC). It investigates whether the influence of interest rates, crude oil prices, unemployment and personal income on housing prices changed after the crisis.

## Research Objectives
- Examine changes in the relationship between housing prices and macroeconomic variables before and after the 2008 financial crisis.
- Compare national-level findings with state-level dynamics across 50 US states.
- Identify changes in the transmission of macroeconomic shocks to housing prices.

## Data
- National monthly data: January 1996 – December 2019.
- State-level quarterly data: 50 US states, 1995 Q4 – 2019 Q4.
- Sources include FRED and FHFA housing price indices.

## Methodology
The analysis employs:
- Vector Autoregression (VAR)
- Phillips–Perron and KPSS stationarity tests
- Zivot–Andrews structural break test
- Granger causality tests
- Impulse Response Functions (IRFs)
- Forecast Error Variance Decomposition (FEVD)

The national analysis compares January 1996–September 2008 with October 2008–December 2019. State-level models compare pre- and post-crisis periods.

## Key Findings
- The contribution of macroeconomic shocks to national housing price inflation increased from approximately 2.24% before the crisis to 14.82% afterwards.
- The contribution of crude oil price shocks increased from 1.17% to 7.84% in the national FEVD.
- At the state level, the influence of oil shocks weakened after the crisis.
- Post-crisis changes varied substantially across states, indicating that national averages can conceal regional differences.

## Key Takeaway
The 2008 financial crisis reshaped the relationship between macroeconomic fundamentals and US housing prices. However, these changes were not uniform across states, highlighting the importance of regional analysis in housing market research.

## Limitations
- State-level VAR models use relatively short quarterly samples.
- Differencing requirements vary across states.
- Results depend partly on the Cholesky ordering and model specification.
- The analysis ends in December 2019.
